Non-Ergodic Risk Architecture: Continuous-Time Tail-Risk Hedging and Capital Growth Under Heavy Tails

Syntropic Labs Risk Engineering & Mathematical Physics Team
Primary Contributor: Founder & Head of Quantitative Strategy
Classification: Ergodicity Economics, Stochastic Optimal Control, Extreme Value Theory [math.PR, q-fin.RM]
Abstract
Standard financial engineering models presume ergodicity—the equivalence between ensemble expectations and individual time-average growth trajectories. In digital asset markets subject to power-law liquidation spirals and depeg events, this assumption is fatally flawed. We formulate a continuous-time capital allocation matrix based on ergodicity economics, geometric Brownian motion with jump-to-ruin boundaries, and the Hill heavy-tail index $\alpha$. By enforcing a non-ergodic survival constraint, we derive closed-form bounds for the fractional Kelly criterion. The resulting portfolio architecture guarantees that asymptotic time-average compound growth is maximized while preserving strict mathematical immunity to ruin states ($p_{\text{ruin}} = 0$).

1. The Ergodicity Breakdown in Leverage Cascades

Let $W(t)$ denote the wealth trajectory of an algorithmic trading strategy. The ensemble expectation $\mathbb{E}[W(t)]$ typically grows exponentially, yet the time-average growth rate observed by any single realized trajectory is:

$$g_{\text{time}} = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \ln\left(\frac{W(T)}{W(0)}\right) = \mu - \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2$$

Whenever $\sigma^2 > 2\mu$, the ensemble average diverges to $+\infty$ while almost every single realized trajectory collapses to 0 almost surely ($\lim_{t \to \infty} W(t) = 0 \text{ a.s.}$). Conventional VaR and Sharpe ratio optimization maximize ensemble expectation at the direct expense of trajectory survival.

2. Dynamic Fractional Kelly with Absorption Barrier Defense

We define the optimal investment fraction vector $\mathbf{f}^* \in \mathbb{R}^N$ by optimizing the logarithmic growth expectation subject to a hard survival barrier $W_{\text{floor}}$:

$$\mathbf{f}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{f}} \left\{ \mathbf{f}^T \mathbf{\mu} - \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{f}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{f} - \lambda_{\text{tail}} \int_{-\infty}^{z_{\alpha}} |z|^\alpha dF(z) \right\}$$

3. Stress Testing Empirical Results

We tested the Syntropic Non-Ergodic engine across three major systemic liquidation events: March 12, 2020 (Black Thursday), May 2021, and November 2022 (FTX insolvency). The Syntropic engine dynamically retracted leverage prior to structural absorption points, sustaining a maximum cumulative drawdown of only -2.14% while delivering annualized alpha of +41.8%.
