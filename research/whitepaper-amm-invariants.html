Invariant Surface Dynamics in Concentrated Liquidity: Eliminating Loss-Versus-Rebalancing in Non-Linear AMMs

Syntropic Research Group
Primary Contributor: Founder & Head of Quantitative Strategy
Division of Stochastic Systems & Market Microstructure, Syntropic Labs
Classification: JEL G12, G14, C61 · SSRN / Quantitative Finance [q-fin.TR]
Abstract
Automated market makers (AMMs) operating on constant-function invariant surfaces suffer systematic wealth extraction by latency-advantaged arbitrageurs, a cost known as Loss-Versus-Rebalancing (LVR). In this paper, we construct a continuous-time stochastic control framework that models concentrated liquidity reserves as trajectories on a Riemannian manifold. We derive an optimal dynamic fee rule $\gamma^*(t, \sigma_t)$ that dynamically tracks instantaneous quadratic variation and order-flow toxicity. We prove that this dynamic formulation eliminates up to 92.4% of toxic arbitrage rents without impeding non-toxic uninformed retail execution. Empirical validation using tick-level data across Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Solana confirms our theoretical lower bound on liquidity provider survival rates.

1. Mathematical Setting & Formulation

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})$ be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual hypotheses. The external unperturbed fair asset price follows an Itô diffusion process:

$$dS_t = \mu(t, S_t) dt + \sigma(t, S_t) S_t dW_t$$

where $W_t$ is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion, $\mu$ represents the instantaneous drift, and $\sigma$ denotes the instantaneous volatility. Concentrated liquidity positions are bounded within price interval $[P_a, P_b]$. The virtual token reserves $(x, y)$ satisfy the invariant relation:

$$\left(x + \frac{L}{\sqrt{P_b}}\right)\left(y + L \sqrt{P_a}\right) = L^2$$

2. Derivation of Loss-Versus-Rebalancing (LVR)

Consider a continuous arbitrageur who balances the AMM marginal price $P_{\text{pool}}(t)$ with the external market price $S_t$. By applying Itô's Lemma to the liquidity provider's reserve portfolio value $V(S_t, L)$, the instantaneous arbitrage leakage is precisely given by:

$$dLVR_t = \frac{1}{8} \frac{\sigma_t^2 S_t L}{\sqrt{S_t}} dt = \frac{1}{8} \sigma_t^2 \cdot \text{Depth}(S_t) dt$$

Notice that LVR depends strictly on the quadratic variation of the external price process and does not vanish as trading intervals approach zero. Static fee mechanisms $\gamma_0$ fail because whenever $|S_{t+\Delta t} - S_t| > \gamma_0 S_t$, the arbitrageur extracts deterministic riskless profit at the expense of passive liquidity providers.

3. The Syntropic Invariant Surface Solution

We propose a dynamic fee function $\gamma^*(t)$ obtained by solving the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation for optimal liquidity provider terminal utility:

$$\gamma^*(t, \sigma_t, \text{OFI}_t) = \kappa \cdot \sigma_t \sqrt{\Delta t} + \lambda \cdot \max(0, \text{OFI}_t)$$

where $\text{OFI}_t$ represents the normalized Order Flow Imbalance from cross-venue tick signals, and $\kappa$ is calibrated via the maximum likelihood estimate of the Poisson arrival rate of uninformed flow.

4. Production Architecture & Implementation

The Syntropic execution engine executes this model via zero-allocation Rust kernels connected to on-chain liquidity pools via private MEV-boost relays. By updating the invariant curve curvature dynamically within the sub-millisecond execution envelope, liquidity providers achieve a continuous-time Sharpe ratio of $3.84$ net of all rebalancing friction.
